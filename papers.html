Githinji & Shah (2025) HINTs: Human-INTuited Cues for Reinforcement Learning

We propose a novel learning framework, called Human-INTuited cues for RL, or HINTs, in which agents quickly learn to solve tasks by leveraging human coaching. Our experiments in classic control, navigation, and locomotion reveal that HINTs enables agents to learn more quickly than vision-only agents and to obtain strategies that apply to more challenging settings.


Githinji & Shah (2023) Adaptive State Representations for Multi-step Deformable Object Manipulation

Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches learn and fix task-specific representations, which places limitations on the agents, which will inevitably encounter task variations. Motivated by recent developments in representation learning and hierarchical RL, in this work we address multi-step deformable object manipulation using adaptive representations in a model-based RL framework. Results from our experiments demonstrate notable performance improvement over fixed-representation baselines in cloth reconfiguration tasks.

Githinji & Shah (2021) Generalising Deformable Object Manipulation by Learning to Learn from Demos

Past research has focused on modelling the geometry and dynamics of deformable objects for planning and control, but some recent techniques have circumvented the challenging object modelling problem by using learning from demonstration (LfD) techniques, like behavioural cloning. While promising, many of these methods fail to scale to real world environments because they demand new training data to generate policies for new task settings. This work emphasises the need to develop methods that quickly learn and transfer the structure of a task for application to human-in-the-loop systems in the real world.


Papalia, Githinji, et al. (2025) A Roadmap for Climate-Relevant Robotics Research

This paper presents a roadmap for climate-relevant robotics research, identifying high-impact opportunities for collaboration between roboticists and experts across climate domains such as energy, the built environment, transportation, industry, land use, and Earth sciences. A central goal of this roadmap is to inspire new research directions and collaboration by highlighting specific, actionable problems at the intersection of robotics and climate. This work represents a collaboration between robotics researchers and domain experts in various climate disciplines, and it serves as an invitation to the robotics community to bring their expertise to bear on urgent climate priorities.
